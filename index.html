The rules of engagement
  • Defeat the opposing fleet by forcing surrender or sinking its ships.
  • Guns train only 15° either side of the beam. Sailing straight at an enemy exposes your bow.
  • A gun-deck rake damages fighting strength along the shot’s path. Stern hits can impair steering.
  • Reloads start at two simulated minutes and slow as crews are disabled.
  • Hulls are solid. Survivors pass disabled hulls and reform their line.
  • The sea has no walls. Edge arrows show ships beyond view.
  • A warning appears 2.8 km from the battle centre. Beyond 3.6 km, return within 12 real seconds or your fleet retreats and loses. Pausing stops the countdown.

Bars beneath ships: hull in fleet colour, fighting strength in gold. Steer away from the wind to regain speed.

History & model limits

Late eighteenth / early nineteenth century sailing combat. Wind, crew losses, solid hulls and limited broadsides change your tactical choices. Pace speeds up sailing, turning and reloads together; it does not make the ships faster in simulated knots.

Damage coefficients, a 60° upwind dead zone, 110 m formation spacing, turn rate, firing arc and 350 m engagement limit are game assumptions. Real outcomes varied with ship design, sea state, gun weight, training and range. No universal raking multiplier is claimed.

Three shot markers represent gun groups. Exact sail handling, leeway, vertical ballistics, smoke obscuration, boarding and damage control are omitted. Struck ships stay fixed; sinking hulls clear immediately. Automatic line recovery and retreat limits are gameplay rules.
